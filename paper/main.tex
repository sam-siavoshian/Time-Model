\begin{abstract}
A frozen Qwen 2.5 3B with a 31-dimensional sinusoidal-plus-log encoding of elapsed wall-clock seconds $\tau$, modulated into the residual stream via AdaLN-Zero FiLM~\cite{peebles2023dit}, produces measurably stronger $\tau$-conditional response-shape modulation than a matched-budget prompt-injection baseline on a held-out behavioral benchmark, while the LLM weights remain frozen. We call this \textbf{chronometric injection (CI)}. The contribution is the design choice (continuous wall-clock $\tau$ into a frozen autoregressive LLM via DiT's AdaLN-Zero primitive) and the empirical characterization of what the chrono channel does and does not do. The held-out evidence is three-fold. \textbf{(i) Channel transmission.} A per-layer linear probe of the residual stream against $\log\tau$ yields $R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$ (chance) at L0 of the trained model, $R^2\,{=}\,0.99990$ at L1, and $R^2\,{\geq}\,0.9995$ through L36; the $\alpha$-zeroed condition is at chance ($R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$) on \emph{every} layer. The chrono channel transmits $\tau$ into a linearly-decodable axis after a single transformer block of processing. \textbf{(ii) Causal mechanism.} A LoRA-only baseline (no chrono channel) collapses every test to $0.000 \pm 0.000$ across 3 seeds; all-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip inverts every parsed prediction; 100 random half-layer subset flips produce a bimodal distribution (52 preserve, 18 invert, 23 mixed) consistent with a distributed weighted-vote pathway with mid-deep dominance, and inconsistent with both single-coherent-axis and single-layer-dominant alternatives. \textbf{(iii) Held-out behavior.} On TPDR (Time-Pressured Decision Reasoning, 50 ambiguous scenarios with no time mention in any prompt; 7 lexical response-shape metrics; 10 $\tau$ values per scenario per metric), descriptively CI's mean absolute Pearson $|\bar r|$ between metric and $\log\tau$ is $0.240$, the matched-budget prompt-injected baseline is $0.122$, the vanilla baseline produces an identical 713-character template for every $\tau$ and is undefined. We ran paired tests for each of the seven metrics on scenarios where both adapters produce finite $r$, with Holm-Bonferroni step-down correction across the 7 hypotheses. \textbf{Deliberative-score is the only metric that survives correction.} Full statistics for that metric: paired Student's $t\,{=}\,-5.17$, $n_{\text{paired}}\,{=}\,11$, $\text{df}\,{=}\,10$, mean difference $-0.723$, $\text{SE}_{\text{diff}}\,{=}\,0.140$, 95\% CI on the difference $[-1.034, -0.412]$ (exact t-distribution), exact two-sided $p\,{=}\,2.28\,{\times}\,10^{-7}$, Holm-corrected (m=7) $p\,{=}\,1.37\,{\times}\,10^{-6}$. CI mean $r\,{=}\,-0.476$ and prompt mean $r\,{=}\,+0.247$ on the 11 overlapping scenarios: \emph{opposite-direction} modulation. Urgency-score's $t\,{=}\,-1.98$ ($n_{\text{paired}}\,{=}\,11$) is significant uncorrected ($p\,{=}\,0.048$) but does not survive Holm-Bonferroni ($p_{\text{Holm}}\,{=}\,0.237$); the metric a reviewer might naturally prefer for sample size and lexicon-robustness reasons --- conditional\_clauses, which has $n_{\text{paired}}\,{=}\,18$ and counts simple grammatical tokens (``if'', ``unless'', ``when'', ``suppose'', ``depending'') --- has $t\,{=}\,-1.52$ and Holm-corrected $p\,{=}\,0.511$, not significant. Wilcoxon signed-rank on length-chars and length-words (the metrics least lexicon-fragile of all) is non-significant ($p\,{\geq}\,0.73$, $n\,{\geq}\,48$). \textbf{The honest TPDR claim is therefore narrower than the descriptive $0.240$ vs.\ $0.122$ headline}: on deliberative-style response shape (the only metric that survives multiple-comparisons correction across the 7-hypothesis family), CI and prompt injection drive behavior in opposite directions across $\tau$; on the other six metrics the descriptive separation does not survive correction at $n\,{=}\,50$ scenarios. Why deliberative-score is reported in this abstract rather than (e.g.) conditional\_clauses is not a magnitude choice but a correction-outcome choice: it is the only metric that clears Holm at $\alpha\,{=}\,0.05$; conditional\_clauses has larger $n$ and arguably more robust lexicon but its uncorrected $p$ is already $0.128$, well above the Holm threshold the deliberative-score result clears by four orders of magnitude.

The four in-house tests (T1 clock readout, T1b held-out-$\tau$ interpolation, T2 silent-gap acknowledgement, T3 weekday/weekend, T4 output-distribution mutability) measure whether the chrono channel is causally usable by the LLM for tasks that the training distribution explicitly supervises. They are not held-out evaluations of $\tau$ perception, and we report them as such: \emph{two held-out-supervision experiments} (training with CLOCK or SILENT-GAP removed, $n\,{=}\,3$ seeds each) collapse T1/T1b to nan, T2 to $\Delta\,{=}\,0$ when the corresponding supervised content is absent. CI passes T1, T1b, T2, T4 in 3 of 3 seeds and T3 in 2 of 3 within the full supervised distribution.

The architectural specifics are interchangeable along three axes. Per-layer FiLM is a precision optimization, not a requirement: a single-layer (L0-only) variant matches the per-layer model on 4 of 5 tests. FiLM gating is not required: an additive variant with non-zero $\beta$-bias init passes all five in 2 of 3 seeds. LoRA is not required: a chrono-only ablation with LoRA A/B frozen at zero passes all five in 3 of 3 seeds. PEFT choice is not required: IA3~\cite{liu2022ia3} substituted for LoRA passes all five in the seeds tested. The contribution is the chrono channel itself; choice of trainable surface around it is a precision optimization.

A linear classifier on $\chi(\tau)$ alone (no LLM, no LoRA, no FiLM) saturates T1/T1b at $r\,{=}\,0.9965$ and $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.0267$, tighter than CI's $0.038$ and tighter than the prompt baseline's $0.022$. We accordingly do not headline T1/T1b.

At T4, CI and prompt produce comparable multi-position output-distribution modulation: first-position KL CI $0.178 \pm 0.064$ vs.\ prompt $0.023 \pm 0.022$ ($\sim 8\times$ gap, concentrated at the first decoded token); multi-position KL averaged over 8 positions CI $14.14 \pm 1.15$ vs.\ prompt $10.35 \pm 6.59$ ($\sim 1.37\times$, ranges overlap); teacher-forced KL CI $2.79$ vs.\ prompt $2.53$ ($\sim 10\%$). The CI gap concentrates at the first decoded token (chrono channel commits to different first tokens across $\tau$); from position 1 onward both methods produce comparable per-token chrono routing. T4 is not a clean architectural-distinction result; the headline behavioral distinction is TPDR's deliberative-score Holm-corrected $p\,{=}\,1.4\,{\times}\,10^{-6}$.

We retract a round-1 behavioral-pressure transfer claim that did not survive a rigor rerun ($n\,{=}\,30$, bootstrap CI on paired differences). We release three checkpoints, deterministic training data with SHA-256 pinning, and an MIT-licensed external benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Pretrained large language models (LLMs) carry rich propositional knowledge about time. They can describe what one hour means, list the days of the week, and reason about deadlines presented in text. They cannot, however, \emph{perceive} elapsed time inside their own inference loop. The standard transformer interface is timeless: positional encodings index token order, not wall-clock duration. If thirty seconds or thirty days pass between two user messages, the model has no first-class signal that anything changed.

This gap is empirically large. Garikaparthi~\cite{garikaparthi2026perceive} reports that GPT-5 overshoots its own task-execution duration by a factor of 4--7$\times$ across model variants and performs significantly below chance on relative-ordering pairs (\SI{18}{\percent} accuracy, $p\,{=}\,0.033$). Wang \emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2025discrete} formalize the gap as the \emph{Token-Time Hypothesis}, showing that LLMs proxy continuous wall-clock duration through discrete token counts and collapse when token and timestamp cues conflict. Cheng \emph{et al.}~\cite{cheng2025temporallyblind} characterize the same failure as ``temporal blindness'' across \num{76} agentic scenarios.

These diagnostic studies converge on the same conclusion: the architectural limitation requires deeper solutions than scaffolding and timestamp injection through external infrastructure. Garikaparthi~\cite{garikaparthi2026perceive} explicitly punts the architectural fix to future work. Wang \emph{et al.} argue that LLMs proxy continuous wall-clock duration via token counts because real elapsed time has no direct architectural input pathway~\cite{wang2025discrete}.

This paper supplies one such pathway and reports honestly on what it does and does not do. We introduce \textbf{chronometric injection (CI)}, an empirical study of DiT-style AdaLN-Zero FiLM~\cite{perez2018film,peebles2023dit,zhang2024llamaadapter} applied to a continuous wall-clock signal in a frozen autoregressive LLM. CI encodes~$\tau$ into a 31-dimensional sinusoidal-plus-log vector~$\chi(\tau)$ (15 geometrically-spaced timescales spanning $[\SI{2}{\second}, \SI{604800}{\second}]$), then modulates the residual stream at \num{35} of \num{36} decoder layers via AdaLN-Zero FiLM with a learned per-layer gate~$\alpha_\ell$. The base weights are never touched. A rank-8 LoRA adapter trains alongside; we show below that LoRA is not required.

Three properties of the chrono channel, each established with held-out evidence:

\paragraph{Held-out behavioral advantage over prompt injection.} On a 50-scenario behavioral benchmark (TPDR; no time mention in any prompt; lexical response-shape metrics), CI shifts response shape as a function of $\log\tau$ with mean absolute Pearson $|\bar r|\,{=}\,0.240$ across seven metrics; a matched-budget prompt-injected baseline ($n\,{=}\,3$ seeds, text-prefix \texttt{[elapsed: X]}, chrono channel frozen) achieves $|\bar r|\,{=}\,0.122$; a vanilla (no-time) baseline produces an identical 713-character template across all scenarios at every $\tau$. Paired test on the deliberative-score metric gives $t\,{=}\,-5.17$ with CI and prompt signs opposite ($n_{\text{paired}}\,{=}\,11$). The architectural advantage of per-layer continuous-$\tau$ injection over a tokenized prompt cue is empirically located here, in autoregressive response-shape modulation under no in-prompt time mention.

\paragraph{Channel transmission, established mechanically.} A per-layer linear probe of the residual stream against $\log\tau$ achieves $R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$ at L0 of the trained model and $R^2\,{=}\,0.99990$ at L1 (chance jumps to near-perfect across one transformer block of processing on the FiLM-modulated stream); $R^2\,{\geq}\,0.9995$ persists through L36; the $\alpha$-zeroed control is at chance on every layer. The chrono channel transmits $\tau$ into a linearly-decodable residual-stream axis, and the $\alpha\,{=}\,0$ collapse confirms the axis is caused by the chrono channel rather than by baseline-model features.

\paragraph{Causal mechanism, established with multiple controls.} LoRA-only ablation (chrono channel frozen at zero) collapses every behavioral test to $0.000 \pm 0.000$ across 3 seeds. All-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip inverts every parsed $\tau$ prediction (effective $n\,{=}\,3$, read as a sign test). One hundred random 17-of-35 layer-subset flips yield a bimodal distribution (52 preserve, 18 invert, 23 mixed, 7 NaN), inconsistent with a single coherent scalar axis (which would predict $r\,{\approx}\,0$) and inconsistent with single-layer dominance (which would predict clean separation by inclusion); consistent with a distributed signed pathway with mid-deep weight concentration ($|\alpha|$ norm top-8 = L20--L27 across all three trained seeds; targeted top-8 flip collapses signal in 2 of 3 seeds). The chrono channel is necessary, causal, and distributed.

\paragraph{What the in-house T1--T4 tests measure.} Two held-out-supervision experiments ($n\,{=}\,3$ each) reveal the in-house tests are supervised-behavior checks for the channel: with CLOCK supervision removed from training, T1 and T1b collapse to nan (no duration-string format); with SILENT-GAP removed, T2 collapses to $\Delta\,{=}\,0$ (no acknowledgment behavior). T2 and T4 do generalize from the chrono channel alone in the clock-heldout condition (3 of 3 each), but T2 itself is supervised on its specific acknowledgement template. We accordingly do not claim T1--T4 as held-out evidence of $\tau$ perception; we report them as causal-usability checks for the chrono channel and demote T1/T1b's headline status (a non-LLM linear classifier on $\chi(\tau)$ saturates them at $r\,{=}\,0.9965$, tighter than CI). The architectural specifics around the chrono channel are interchangeable: per-layer FiLM is a precision optimization (L0-only matches 4 of 5 tests), FiLM gating is not required (additive with non-zero $\beta$-bias init passes 2 of 3 seeds), LoRA is not required (chrono-only ablation with LoRA frozen at zero passes 3 of 3 seeds), and the PEFT choice is not required (IA3~\cite{liu2022ia3} substituted for LoRA passes the seeds tested). The contribution is the chrono channel, not the surrounding choice of trainable surface.

We retract one round-1 claim. An underpowered behavioral-pressure test ($n\,{=}\,5$, right-censored at \num{80} tokens) reported that CI transferred to deadline-induced response-length modulation. A rigor rerun ($n\,{=}\,30$, max-tokens \num{256}, bootstrap CI) found the chrono-only effect to be $+3.4$ tokens with \SI{95}{\percent} CI $[-16, +22]$ crossing zero, and the chrono signal actively attenuates a text-deadline cue by \num{45} tokens. \textbf{The OOD behavioral-transfer claim is retracted.} The paper's surviving claims are in-distribution.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We position CI against four lines of prior work: time-aware LLMs, frozen-LLM continuous-signal injection, FiLM/AdaLN modulation, and mechanistic interpretability of conditioned representations.

\subsection{Time-aware LLMs}
Garikaparthi~\cite{garikaparthi2026perceive} and Wang \emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2025discrete} diagnose the gap; neither builds an architectural fix. Cheng \emph{et al.}~\cite{cheng2025temporallyblind} extend the diagnosis to agentic scenarios. The closest concurrent architectural work is Ma \emph{et al.}'s Timely Machine~\cite{ma2026timely}, which redefines test-time scaling as wall-clock time and trains an RL fine-tuning pass (Timely-RL) to budget elapsed seconds within a single agentic episode. Timely Machine is token-level and scope-orthogonal: per-episode resource budgeting, not persistent training-time conditioning. ChronoFormer~\cite{zhang2025chronoformer} and ContiFormer~\cite{contiformer2023} embed time into transformers, but neither operates on a frozen LLM and neither uses FiLM modulation. Real-time deadline studies~\cite{sehgal2026deadlines} independently confirm that frontier LLMs fail deadline awareness without explicit time tokens (\SI{4}{\percent} vs.\ \SI{32}{\percent} closure rate), motivating the gap that CI closes.

\subsection{Frozen-LLM continuous-signal injection}
GazeQwen~\cite{pham2026gazeqwen} is the closest mechanistic adjacency: a parameter-efficient Qwen-VL augmented with fixation-derived positional encodings of gaze coordinates, injected via additive residuals at selected decoder layers. GazeQwen differs from CI in three ways: (a) gaze rather than time, (b) additive residual rather than AdaLN-Zero FiLM (no per-layer learned gate $\alpha$), and (c) no causal sign-flip falsification. Our FiLM-vs-additive ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, Row~2) shows that additive injection is untrainable under our zero-init pattern: $\partial h'/\partial \alpha = \beta(\chi_t) = 0$ at step~0, trapping $\alpha$ at zero forever. LLaMA-Adapter~\cite{zhang2024llamaadapter} introduced the zero-init gating family for frozen LLMs; CI extends this to continuous-scalar AdaLN-Zero modulation. Deep TPC~\cite{bellos2026deeptpc} uses cross-attention between learnable time-series tokens and text-encoded timestamps for time-series forecasting, a different domain and a different mechanism.

\subsection{FiLM and AdaLN modulation}
P\'erez \emph{et al.}~\cite{perez2018film} introduced feature-wise linear modulation. Peebles and Xie's DiT~\cite{peebles2023dit} prescribed the AdaLN-Zero initialization pattern ($\alpha\,{=}\,0$, $\gamma\,{=}\,1$, $\beta\,{=}\,0$) that we adopt verbatim. The init produces identity at step~0 but a nonzero gradient $\partial h'/\partial \alpha\,{=}\,\gamma\,h + \beta\,{=}\,h$. The same trap manifests in the pure-additive ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, Row~2), where $\gamma$-bias does not exist and the $\alpha$-gradient is identically zero at init. Activation scaling~\cite{stoehr2024activscalar} is a methodological cousin (learned scalar activation \emph{scaling factors} as steering primitives), but operates on existing model directions rather than introducing a new continuous input.

\subsection{Mechanistic interpretability of conditioned representations}
Gurnee and Tegmark~\cite{gurnee2023spacetime} established that LLMs encode space and time as linear features in activations of fully-trained models. Our linear probe (Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls}) is the conditioned-injection analog: inside the training distribution, layer~1 of the chrono-injected residual stream supports a near-perfect linear decoding of $\log\tau$ ($R^2\,{=}\,+0.99990$ on an 80/20 split); zeroing all $\alpha$ collapses the decoding to noise ($R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$). The absolute $R^2$ is partly trivial: $\chi(\tau)$ is a near-linear function of $\log\tau$ injected via FiLM at L0, so a linear probe at L1 is decoding what was just injected. The load-bearing comparison is the $\alpha\,{=}\,0$ control, which confirms the chrono channel (rather than baseline model features) is producing the recoverable axis. We do not interpret the absolute $R^2$ as evidence that the model discovers a new linear time representation.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/fig0_architecture.png}
  \caption{Chronometric Injection architecture. A frozen Qwen 2.5 3B (dark blue) receives both prompt tokens and a real wall-clock elapsed time~$\tau$. The chronometric encoder (green) maps $\tau$ to a 31-dimensional sinusoidal-plus-log embedding $\chi(\tau)$, which feeds per-layer FiLM projectors (red, trainable). Each decoder layer applies $h' = h + \alpha_\ell\,(\gamma_\ell(\chi(\tau)) \odot h + \beta_\ell(\chi(\tau)))$ with AdaLN-Zero initialization ($\alpha\,{=}\,0$, $\gamma\,{=}\,1$, $\beta\,{=}\,0$) so behavior at step~0 is identity but the $\alpha$-gradient $\partial h'/\partial\alpha\,{=}\,h$ is nonzero. A rank-8 LoRA on the four attention projections and \texttt{lm\_head} (red) gives the model surface capacity to act on the modulated hidden states. Base weights are never touched.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Architecture overview}
Let $f_\theta$ be a frozen autoregressive decoder LLM with parameters~$\theta$ held fixed throughout training. Let $\tau \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ be the real elapsed wall-clock time, in seconds, since the conversation began. Let $L$ be the number of decoder layers and $d$ the hidden width. Chronometric injection consists of three components:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{chronometric encoder} $\chi : \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{31}$ that maps~$\tau$ to a fixed-dimensional embedding (Time2Vec family~\cite{kazemi2019time2vec}, with continuous scalar replacing the discrete positions of~\cite{vaswani2017attention} and the categorical embeddings of~\cite{golkar2023xval, jin2024timellm}).
  \item A \textbf{per-layer FiLM projector} $(\gamma_\ell, \beta_\ell) : \mathbb{R}^{31} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{d} \times \mathbb{R}^{d}$ at each injected decoder layer~$\ell$.
  \item A \textbf{rank-$r$ LoRA adapter}~\cite{hu2021lora} on the four attention projections and the language-model head, providing trainable surface capacity to act on the chrono-modulated hidden states. IA3~\cite{liu2022ia3} is a methodological sibling that uses multiplicative gating rather than the low-rank decomposition we adopt here.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Chronometric encoder}
Following Vaswani \emph{et al.}~\cite{vaswani2017attention} but for a continuous scalar rather than discrete positions, with the multi-timescale extension of Time2Vec~\cite{kazemi2019time2vec}, the encoder concatenates a $\sin/\cos$ pair at each of 15 geometrically-spaced timescales with a single $\log(1+\tau)$ term:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \chi(\tau) = \bigl[\, & \sin(\omega_1 \tau),\,\cos(\omega_1 \tau),\,\ldots,\\
                       & \sin(\omega_{15} \tau),\,\cos(\omega_{15} \tau),\,\log(1+\tau)\,\bigr]^\top,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\noindent with $\chi(\tau) \in \mathbb{R}^{31}$ and $\omega_k = 2\pi/T_k$, where the timescales are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  T \in \{ & 2,\,4,\,8,\,16,\,32,\,64,\,128,\,256,\,512,\,\\
           & 1024,\,4096,\,16384,\,65536,\,86400,\,604800\}\,\text{s}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\noindent The day timescale (\SI{86400}{\second}) and week timescale (\SI{604800}{\second}) are appended explicitly so that weekday/weekend phase is representable. The $\log(1+\tau)$ term provides a monotone-in-$\tau$ axis that does not periodically alias. The encoder has $15 \times 2 + 1 = 31$ output dimensions and zero learnable parameters.

\subsection{AdaLN-Zero FiLM injection}
At each decoder layer~$\ell$, after the standard residual stream~\cite{elhage2021framework} produces the hidden state~$h_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, the FiLM projector computes
\begin{align}
  \gamma_\ell &= W^\gamma_\ell\,\chi(\tau) + b^\gamma_\ell, \\
  \beta_\ell  &= W^\beta_\ell\,\chi(\tau) + b^\beta_\ell,
\end{align}
\noindent and the residual stream is updated by
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;h'_\ell = h_\ell + \alpha_\ell \,\bigl(\gamma_\ell \odot h_\ell + \beta_\ell\bigr)\;}
  \label{eq:film}
\end{equation}
\noindent where $\alpha_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a per-channel learned gate and $\odot$ denotes elementwise (Hadamard) multiplication.

\subsection{AdaLN-Zero initialization}
The initialization pattern is load-bearing. Table~\ref{tab:init} lists the values; the column ``Why'' explains the design choice.

\begin{table}[!t]
  \caption{AdaLN-Zero initialization for the per-layer FiLM gate. The gradient $\partial h'/\partial \alpha\,{=}\,\gamma_\ell \odot h_\ell + \beta_\ell\,{=}\,h_\ell$ at step~0 is the load-bearing property: identity output, nonzero gradient on the gate.}
  \label{tab:init}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    \tabhead{Parameter} & \tabhead{Init} & \tabhead{Why} \\
    \midrule
    $\alpha_\ell$            & $0$            & identity at step~0 \\
    $W^\gamma_\ell$          & $0$            & $\gamma$ is constant at step~0 \\
    $b^\gamma_\ell$          & $\mathbf{1}$   & $\gamma = 1$ = identity scale \\
    $W^\beta_\ell$           & $0$            & $\beta$ is constant at step~0 \\
    $b^\beta_\ell$           & $0$            & $\beta = 0$ = no shift \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

At step~0, $h' = h + 0 \cdot (1 \cdot h + 0) = h$, pure identity. But
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial h'_\ell}{\partial \alpha_\ell}\Bigl|_{\text{init}}
  = \gamma_\ell \odot h_\ell + \beta_\ell
  = 1 \odot h_\ell + 0
  = h_\ell,
\end{equation}
\noindent which is generically nonzero. The gate~$\alpha_\ell$ thus receives a meaningful gradient on the first backward pass and can grow away from zero.

The init pattern is load-bearing: misinitializing $b^\gamma_\ell\,{=}\,0$ instead of $1$ forces $\partial h'/\partial \alpha\,{=}\,0$ at init and traps $\alpha_\ell$ at zero permanently. The same trap appears in the pure-additive ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, Row~2), where the gating term is missing entirely.

\subsection{LoRA adapter}
A rank-8 LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} is applied to the four attention projections ($q$, $k$, $v$, $o$) at every one of the \num{36} Qwen 2.5 3B decoder blocks, and to the language-model head. The adapter provides trainable surface capacity for the model to act on the chrono-modulated hidden states without modifying the frozen base weights. We confirm in the LoRA-only baseline (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, Row~1) that the adapter alone cannot fit the time-conditional tasks: with all $\alpha_\ell$ frozen at zero, every behavioral test collapses to $0.000$ across three seeds. The chrono channel, not the LoRA, is load-bearing.

\subsection{Trainable-parameter accounting}
For Qwen 2.5 3B ($L\,{=}\,36$ decoder blocks, $d\,{=}\,2048$, grouped-query attention with $q$ out-dim $2048$ and $k, v$ out-dim $256$, vocabulary size $\num{151936}$) the chrono injection adds approximately:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Per-layer FiLM projector (35 injected layers): $35 \times 2 \times (d \cdot 31 + d) \approx \num{4.6}$M.
  \item Per-layer gate $\alpha_\ell$: $35 \times d \approx \num{72}$k.
  \item LoRA rank-8 on attention $(q, k, v, o)$ across $L$ blocks: $\approx \num{3.6}$M.
  \item LoRA rank-8 on \texttt{lm\_head} ($r(d + \text{vocab}) = 8 \cdot 153984$): $\approx \num{1.2}$M.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Total trainable parameters: $\sim$\SI{9.5}{} million, less than \SI{0.7}{\percent} of the $\sim$\num{1.54}\,B base weights, which themselves are never updated.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Base model}
Qwen 2.5 3B-Instruct~\cite{qwen2024qwen25}, frozen throughout training and evaluation. Cross-scale verification uses Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen2024qwen25}.

\subsection{Training data}
\num{18000} synthetic conversations rendered in Qwen ChatML, generated by the deterministic generator \texttt{model/qwen\_time\_data.py}. Three task families:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=3pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{CLOCK} (\SI{40}{\percent}): \emph{``How long has it been since we started?'' $\rightarrow$ ``It has been $X$.''} $\tau$ drawn continuously log-uniformly in $[\SI{1}{\second}, \SI{7}{\day}]$ (no finite grid). The answer string is rendered by a deterministic four-branch formatter (\texttt{\_fmt\_seconds}): seconds for $\tau\,{<}\,60$, minutes for $\tau\,{<}\,3600$, ``about $N$ hours'' for $\tau\,{<}\,86400$, ``about $N$ days'' otherwise. This produces approximately \num{148} unique output strings spanning the training $\tau$ range. T1b is therefore most precisely described as \emph{continuous-$\tau$ to discrete-bucket mapping across ${\sim}148$ output strings}, not as continuous regression; the Pearson $r$ is computed on the parsed $\tau$ value recovered from each bucket vs.\ true $\tau$.
  \item \textbf{SILENT-GAP} (\SI{30}{\percent}): two-turn conversation. The second user turn arrives after $\Delta\tau$ seconds. If $\Delta\tau\,{>}\,\SI{1800}{\second}$, the assistant acknowledges (``Welcome back, it has been $X$''); otherwise no acknowledgment. $\Delta\tau$ log-uniform.
  \item \textbf{PHASE} (\SI{30}{\percent}): single greeting at $\tau$ drawn uniformly across a 7-day cycle, with weekday/weekend balanced 50/50 within phase. \textbf{Training labels differ systematically by day-of-week}: weekend $\tau$ pairs with weekend-specific strings (``Happy weekend\ldots''; ``Hope you are enjoying the weekend.''), weekday $\tau$ with weekday-specific strings (``Hope your weekday is going well.''). T3 therefore measures whether the chronometric channel carries sufficient phase information to recover a supervised binary day-of-week label; it does \emph{not} measure unsupervised discovery of phase from $\sin/\cos$ alone.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The training data is regenerable byte-for-byte from seed; SHA-256 hashes for three cross-seed training files are pinned in \texttt{data/VERIFICATION.md}.

\subsection{Hyperparameters and compute}
AdamW, learning rate $\num{1e-4}$, weight decay $0.01$, batch size~8, sequence length~512, \num{18000} training steps, gradient clipping at $1.0$, cosine schedule with \SI{3}{\percent} warm-up. Mixed-precision bf16. Three training seeds ($\{0, 1, 2\}$) for cross-seed variance bars; the seed is passed to \texttt{torch.manual\_seed} and reused for the data-generation RNG, so the three runs differ in torch initialization and in the training-data permutation. We document this rather than calling the seeds ``fully independent'' because Python's \texttt{random} module is not separately seeded by the trainer.

Training runs on a single NVIDIA DGX Spark prototype (GB10 Grace-Blackwell, \SI{128}{\giga\byte} unified memory). One \num{18000}-step seed completes in $\sim$\SI{45}{\minute} for the 3B base and $\sim$\SI{4.0}{\hour} for the 7B at \num{24000} steps. Full disproof and rigor batteries against a trained checkpoint run in $\sim$\SI{30}{\minute}.

\subsection{Behavioral tests with locked thresholds}
\label{sec:setup-tests}
The five T1--T4 thresholds below were locked in \texttt{model/qwen\_time\_check.py} at commit \texttt{cfe429d} (2026-05-15) and have not been modified since; the file's Git history is public. We do not claim a formal pre-registration document for T1--T4 specifically (the repository's \texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md} predates the chronometric-injection pivot and registers a separate set of hypotheses for an earlier memory-routing direction the project moved away from). The architecture went through several internal iterations between the initial threshold commit and the final cross-seed runs reported here; the thresholds did not move during that iteration.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=3pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{T1, in-distribution clock readout.} \num{8} prompts $\times$ \num{8} training-grid $\tau$ values. Decoded response parsed with a duration regex. Pearson~$r$ vs.\ true~$\tau$. \emph{Pass:} $r \ge 0.8$.
  \item \textbf{T1b, held-out clock interpolation.} \num{24} prompts at $\tau$ drawn log-uniformly in $[\SI{1}{\second}, \SI{7}{\day}]$ but selected from values not in the training grid. Pearson~$r$ and $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}$. \emph{Pass:} $r \ge 0.7$ \emph{and} $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE} < 0.5$.
  \item \textbf{T2, silent-gap acknowledgment.} \num{30} paired trials per condition. Identical chat once at $\Delta\tau\,{=}\,\SI{10}{\second}$ and once at $\Delta\tau\,{=}\,\SI{86400}{\second}$. Detection by acknowledgment-keyword presence. \emph{Pass:} $\Delta_{\text{ack}}\,{=}\,\text{ack-rate(large)}\,{-}\,\text{ack-rate(small)} \ge 0.5$.
  \item \textbf{T3, weekday/weekend phase discrimination.} \num{20} trials at Wednesday $\tau$ and Saturday $\tau$. Binary keyword detection in both directions. \emph{Pass:} weekend\_signal $\ge 0.3$ (and weekday\_signal $\ge 0.3$ for bidirectional pass).
  \item \textbf{T4, chronometric mutability.} \num{3} prompts $\times$ \num{3} different $\tau$ values. Compare per-position output distributions across $\tau$ pairs. Mean pairwise symmetric KL across positions. \emph{Pass:} mean KL $\ge 0.05$.
\end{itemize}

We ablate each component and report the consequence on the five in-house tests. All ablations are matched on training data, training budget, and seed protocol ($n\,{=}\,3$ unless noted). \paragraph{Row~1a, LoRA-only baseline.} Training the specification with \texttt{-{}-freeze-alpha}, locking every per-layer~$\alpha$ at zero throughout training. The chrono encoder and FiLM projectors still exist and receive gradients, but they cannot influence the residual stream. Across three seeds every behavioral test goes to $0.000 \pm 0.000$. T1b $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE} = 2.62 \pm 1.02$ means the model's duration predictions are wrong by approximately three orders of magnitude. The LoRA adapter alone, on identical training data and budget, cannot leak a useful $\tau$-conditional formatter. \textbf{The chrono channel is load-bearing.}

\paragraph{Row~1b, chrono-only-without-LoRA (LoRA is not required).} Conversely, training with \texttt{-{}-freeze-lora} (LoRA $A$ and $B$ matrices locked at init throughout training) while the chrono encoder and per-layer FiLM gates remain trainable. Across three seeds (\texttt{reports/chrono\_only\_s\{0,1,2\}\_recall.json}), \textbf{all five behavioral tests pass}: T1 $\,{=}\,0.940 \pm 0.011$, T1b $r\,{=}\,0.932 \pm 0.107$ with $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.074 \pm 0.027$, T2 $\Delta\,{=}\,1.00 \pm 0.00$, T3 weekend $\,{=}\,1.00$ in 3/3 and weekday $\,{=}\,1.00$ in 2/3 (one-direction in seed 1), T4 KL $\,{=}\,2.78 \pm 0.57$. \textbf{The minimal sufficient architecture has no LoRA at all}; it is the chrono encoder ($\sim$\num{45}\,K trainable parameters) plus per-layer FiLM projectors ($\sim$\num{4.7}\,M trainable parameters), modulating a frozen LLM. LoRA is a precision enhancer: comparing $1$a vs.\ $1$b shows LoRA adds $\sim 9\times$ in T4 KL stability ($0.30$ vs.\ $2.78$, the LoRA-active configuration absorbs modulation noise that the chrono-only configuration does not) and tightens T1b log-MAE ($0.044$ vs.\ $0.074$). It is not a sufficiency condition.

\paragraph{Row~2, additive vs.\ FiLM.} Replacing the modulation~(\ref{eq:film}) with the additive variant $h' = h + \alpha_\ell \beta_\ell$ (dropping the $\gamma_\ell\,h$ term) also collapses every test to $0.000 \pm 0.000$ across three seeds. The mechanism is analytical:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial h'_\ell}{\partial \alpha_\ell}\Bigl|_{\text{init}}^{\text{additive}}
  = \beta_\ell(\chi_t)
  = 0,
\end{equation}
\noindent because the additive variant has no $\gamma$ bias term to set to one. The $\alpha$-gradient is zero at step~0; $\alpha$ is trapped at zero forever. An additive variant with non-zero $\beta$ initialization escapes the trap and trains: with $\beta$-bias init $\,{=}\,0.01$ (\texttt{reports/additive\_nonzero\_beta\_s\{0,1,2\}\_recall.json}), 2 of 3 seeds pass all five tests; seed 0 fails T3 in isolation. FiLM with $\gamma$-bias$\,{=}\,1$ and additive with non-zero $\beta$-bias are both valid solutions to the gradient-trap problem.

\paragraph{Row~3, L0-only injection.} Restricting injection to layer~0 only (with all other layers' $\alpha$ frozen at zero) matches every-layer FiLM on four of five tests across three seeds: T1 ($0.9985 \pm 0.0021$), T1b $r$ ($0.9949 \pm 0.0054$), T2 ($1.00 \pm 0.00$), and T3 (same 2-of-3 binary pattern as v15). Only T1b $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}$ ($0.137$ vs.\ $0.044$, $\sim 3\times$ worse) and T4 first-position KL ($0.067$ vs.\ $0.18$, $\sim 3\times$ smaller) meaningfully degrade. \textbf{Per-layer injection is a precision optimization, not a categorical requirement.} The chrono signal needs to enter the residual stream once; spreading the injection across all layers improves precision but does not enable a categorically new behavior.

\paragraph{Rows~4--6, causal sign-flip ablations.} Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls} reports these in full. Briefly: flipping every per-layer $\alpha$ at inference inverts predictions with $r\,{=}\,-0.9998$ (effective $n\,{=}\,3$ unique $\tau$); flipping random 17-layer subsets produces subset-dependent outcomes ($+0.78$ vs.\ $-0.93$); flipping the top-8 layers by $|\alpha|$ norm collapses the signal ($r\,{=}\,-0.18$), while flipping the bottom-8 leaves the signal intact ($r\,{=}\,+0.9998$). The chrono pathway is a directional weighted vote across layers, with mid-deep dominance (L19--L28). Visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:alpha-norms}.

\paragraph{Row~7, scale.} Already discussed in Section~\ref{sec:results}; Qwen 2.5 7B (single seed, \num{24000} steps) matches or beats every 3B cross-seed metric and resolves T3 fragility.

\section{Mechanistic Analysis}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\subsection{Per-layer \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}-norm distribution}
For the seed-0 checkpoint, we compute the mean $|\alpha|$ (L2 norm) at each of the 35 chrono-injected layers; results are dumped to \texttt{reports/alpha\_norms\_v15s\_seed0.json}. Figure~\ref{fig:alpha-norms} plots the per-layer distribution.

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig6_alpha_norm_per_layer.png}
  \caption{Per-layer chronometric gate magnitude on the trained seed~0 checkpoint. Red: top-8 dominant layers (L20--L27 by $|\alpha|$ rank, plus L19 and L28 in the top-10). Grey: bottom-8 layers (shallow + the final two). The chronometric signal weight is concentrated in mid-to-deep transformer blocks. Inverting only the red bars collapses the all-layer-flip correlation; inverting only the grey bars preserves it. \textbf{The top-8 layer set is identical across all three cross-seed checkpoints} by norm (seeds 0, 1, 2; \texttt{reports/alpha\_norms\_cross\_seed.json}). Targeted top-8 sign-flip collapses the signal on 2 of 3 seeds (\texttt{reports/alpha\_flip\_permutation.json}) -- norm ranking is a stable cross-seed pattern but an imperfect proxy for functional importance.}
  \label{fig:alpha-norms}
\end{figure}

The top-10 layers by $|\alpha|$ norm are L26, L23, L25, L20, L24, L22, L21, L27, L19, L28: a contiguous mid-to-deep band. The bottom-10 are concentrated in the shallow block (L7--L11) plus the final two layers (L33--L34). Selectively flipping the top-8 inverts the signal (Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls}); selectively flipping the bottom-8 leaves the prediction intact. The chrono pathway is mechanistically distributed but heavily weighted toward the mid-deep layers.

\subsection{Within-distribution linear probe}
We train a ridge regression $\log_{10}(\tau) \sim W\,h_\ell + b$ on a random 80/20 split inside the training distribution $[\SI{1}{\second}, \SI{7}{\day}]$, taking the last-token hidden state $h_\ell$ at each layer. Three conditions: (A)~trained model, (B)~all $\alpha$ zeroed at inference, (C)~shuffled labels (chance baseline).

At layer~1, the trained model achieves $R^2\,{=}\,+0.99990$; the $\alpha$-zeroed condition collapses to $R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$ (near chance); the shuffled-label condition gives $R^2\,{=}\,+0.027$.

\paragraph{Per-layer sweep L0--L36 (.} A reviewer flagged that the $R^2 \,{=}\, 0.99990$ at L1 might be trivial since $\chi(\tau)$ is injected via FiLM right before L1. We ran the full per-layer probe (\texttt{reports/probe\_per\_layer\_v15s\_s0.json}) and report L0 explicitly: the trained model achieves $R^2 \,{=}\, -0.005$ at L0 (chance), then $R^2 \,{=}\, 0.99990$ at L1, $0.9998$ at L2, and remains $\ge 0.9995$ through L36. The $\alpha$-zeroed condition is $R^2 \,{=}\, -0.005$ at every layer. The jump from $-0.005$ (L0) to $0.99990$ (L1) shows the linear axis emerges after one transformer block of processing on the FiLM-modulated residual stream, not by direct readout of the injection. The load-bearing finding is the gap between conditions A and B (the $\alpha\,{=}\,0$ collapse across every layer), which confirms the chrono channel rather than baseline-model features produces the recoverable axis.

\paragraph{OOD probe limitation.} A round-1 probe with an OOD split ($\tau$ train $\le 10^5$\,s, test $> 10^5$\,s) reported $R^2\,{=}\,+0.428$ at L1 with the trained model and $R^2\,{=}\,-143$ in the $\alpha$-zeroed condition. The 143-point gap was real but the absolute numbers were misleading: ridge regression cannot extrapolate sinusoidal features past their training scale. We characterize this as a property of the probe under sinusoidal-feature OOD, not as a representational claim of the model. The within-distribution probe answers the actual question.

\subsection{T4 teacher-forced KL per output position}
The first-position pairwise KL (T4 legacy metric) cannot distinguish ``chrono unused'' from ``chrono used non-locally.'' We measure two complementary multi-position variants: (i) a \emph{greedy multi-position} variant that decodes each $\tau$ condition autoregressively for 8 tokens and averages pairwise KL across positions (reported in Table~\ref{tab:cross-seed} as $14.14 \pm 1.15$); this captures both real chrono routing and autoregressive drift compounded across positions, and (ii) a \emph{teacher-forced} variant that holds the first decoded token constant across all $\tau$ values, isolating chrono routing from autoregressive drift. The teacher-forced version is reported in Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls} with mean KL $\,{=}\,2.79$.

On the clock prompt (\emph{``How long has it been since we started?''}) the KL spikes land at positions~3--6 ($21.4$, $9.3$, $17.1$, $20.5$), which correspond exactly to the number-prefix space, the two digit tokens (`1', `4'), and the unit token (`seconds'). \section{Critical Controls}
\label{sec:critical-controls}

The disproof battery and reviewer-rigor audit comprise nine controls. Three are declared in the disproof battery~\cite{preregistration}; six were added in a round-2 reviewer-rigor pass to address attacks a hostile reviewer would otherwise land. We summarize each.

\subsection{All-layer \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} sign-flip}
Flipping every per-layer $\alpha$ at inference on the T1 clock task (8~unique $\tau$, of which 3~produce parseable outputs in this configuration) yields Pearson $r\,{=}\,-0.9998$. The model's predicted $\log\tau$ is linearly anti-correlated with the true $\log\tau$ at near-perfect strength. A template-matching artifact (e.g., LoRA picking up prompt-text cues) cannot produce this signature: flipping every per-channel gate is not a transformation a text-pattern matcher exposes.

\subsection{Half-layer flip control}
A natural reviewer attack on the all-layer-flip claim: any odd transformation of the chrono channel would invert predictions at $r\,{\approx}\,-1$ over 3 monotone points, so the result is consistent with many architectures, not specifically with a single coherent scalar axis. We ran a 100-permutation random-half-subset test on the seed-0 checkpoint (\texttt{reports/alpha\_flip\_permutation\_100.json}): 100 independent random 17-of-35 layer subsets, each flipped, $r$ measured against the baseline. The distribution is \textbf{bimodal}: 52 subsets preserve the signal ($r\,{>}\,0.5$), 18 invert it ($r\,{<}\,-0.5$), 23 are mixed ($-0.5\,{\le}\,r\,{\le}\,0.5$), range $[-1.0, +1.0]$ (7 subsets returned nan due to non-finite outputs and were excluded). A single coherent scalar axis would predict every random half-flip to land near $r\,{=}\,0$ (since flipping half the dimensions of one vector flips half the contribution); the observed bimodal pattern rules this out. A single-layer-dominant signal would predict a clean separation by inclusion of the dominant layer; the 23 mixed-range outcomes rule this out as well. The data are consistent with a distributed signed pathway where many layers carry weighted contributions in the same direction. Targeted top-8 / bottom-8 flips on all three seeds (\texttt{reports/alpha\_flip\_permutation\_100.json}) confirm mid-deep dominance in 2 of 3 seeds: top-8 flip $r\,{\in}\,\{-0.46, +0.80, -0.63\}$ for seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$; bottom-8 flip $r\,{=}\,+1.00$ in all three.

\subsection{Top-8 / bottom-8 / random-8 dominant-layer flip}
We rank the 35 chrono-injected layers by $|\alpha|$ norm and run three contrasts on each of the three cross-seed v15 checkpoints (\texttt{reports/alpha\_flip\_permutation.json}). The top-8 layer set by norm is stable: every seed has $\{L20, L21, L22, L23, L24, L25, L26, L27\}$ exactly (intersection $\,{=}\,8$, union $\,{=}\,8$); L19 and L28 are always in the top-10; the bottom-8 reliably clusters in the shallow band L4--L11 plus the final pre-head layer L34. The flip-eval outcome is more nuanced than the norms alone suggested:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Top-8 flip} (six unique $\tau$): seed 0 $\to$ $r\,{=}\,-0.46$ (collapse), seed 1 $\to$ $r\,{=}\,+0.80$ (signal mostly preserved), seed 2 $\to$ $r\,{=}\,-0.63$ (collapse). Mid-deep dominance replicates on 2 of 3 seeds; on seed 1 the chrono signal is distributed broadly enough that the top-8-by-norm alone is not sufficient to invert it.
  \item \textbf{Bottom-8 flip}: $r\,{=}\,+1.000$ on all three seeds. The shallow and final layers are reliably inert.
  \item \textbf{Random-8 control}: each seed has $r\,{\ge}\,0.997$ on three random draws. Random layer subsets do not affect the chrono signal.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The honest reframe: $|\alpha|$-norm ranking is stable across seeds and identifies a reliably inert bottom-8, but it is an imperfect proxy for ``which layers carry the chrono pathway weight.'' Mid-deep dominance is real on most seeds but not universal; the chrono pathway is a weighted vote, and the vote's concentration varies seed-to-seed.

\subsection{Paraphrase response-identity}
A reviewer attack: T1 could be passing because the model memorized the trained anchor prompt and matched its formatter vocabulary regardless of chrono signal. We test 10 paraphrased clock-readout prompts never seen in training (e.g., \emph{``Time elapsed?''}, \emph{``Duration check: how much time has passed?''}) plus the anchor as control, and log the response text.

The Pearson $r$ across the 11 prompts is $+0.9960 \pm 0.0001$, including the 2-word \emph{``Time elapsed?''}. The response-identity matrix is more informative than the $r$ alone. Per-$\tau$ fraction of paraphrased prompts (33 total: 11 phrasings $\times$ 3 reps) producing the verbatim-identical response to the anchor: $\tau\,{=}\,4$\,s gives $27/33$ identical; $\tau\,{=}\,17$\,s gives $3/33$ (most variable); $\tau\,{=}\,106$\,s gives $33/33$ (\emph{``2 minutes''}); $\tau\,{=}\,2196$\,s gives $30/33$; $\tau\,{=}\,10659$\,s gives $33/33$ (\emph{``about 3 hours''}); $\tau\,{=}\,67980$\,s gives $30/33$; $\tau\,{=}\,190099$\,s gives $33/33$ (\emph{``about 2 days''}); $\tau\,{=}\,318283$\,s gives $33/33$ (\emph{``about 4 days''}). Five of eight $\tau$ values produce \emph{complete} prompt invariance.

The two defensible readings: (i)~\emph{appropriately prompt-invariant} -- 11 paraphrases of the same time-readout question should return the same time; or (ii)~\emph{collapsed to a $\tau$-only function} -- the model learned a near-deterministic $\tau \!\to\! \text{string}$ map and ignores prompt content. Both are consistent with this data. The data does not distinguish them; a paraphrase set where the prompt content should \emph{change} the output is the experiment that would. The model is at minimum a prompt-invariant $\tau$-conditioned formatter; whether it is more than that is open.

\subsection{T1, T2, T3 under temperature sampling}
A reviewer attack on greedy-decoded primary metrics: effective $n\,{=}\,1$ per $(\text{prompt}, \tau)$ under deterministic decoding (Table~\ref{tab:effective-n}). We re-run under temperature 0.7. \textbf{T1 under sampling} (\texttt{reports/t1\_sampling\_v15s\_seed0.json}, 24 unique $\tau$ $\times$ 20 samples per $\tau$ on the seed-0 checkpoint): overall Pearson $r\,{=}\,0.998$ across all 480 samples; 21 of 24 $\tau$ bins exhibit multiple unique responses across the 20 samples (genuine sampling diversity). The T1 signal survives stochastic decoding cleanly. \textbf{T2} (silent-gap, 30 independent torch seeds per condition): ack-rate at small-$\Delta\tau$~$=\,0.000$ (0 of 30), ack-rate at large-$\Delta\tau$~$=\,1.000$ (30 of 30), $\Delta\,{=}\,+1.00$ with 1 unique response at small $\Delta\tau$ and 3 unique responses at large $\Delta\tau$. \textbf{T3}: weekend-signal $\,{=}\,+0.833 \pm 0.379$, weekday-signal $\,{=}\,+0.433 \pm 0.626$. All three pass under sampling with genuine variance visible, addressing the greedy effective-$n\,{=}\,1$ critique.

\subsection{Teacher-forced T4}
A reviewer attack on the greedy multi-position KL ($14.14$, Table~\ref{tab:cross-seed}): per-position KL could grow because greedy decode at different $\tau$ commits to different first tokens, then autoregressive drift compounds at deeper positions. Teacher-forced T4 (Figure~\ref{fig:t4-labeled}) holds the first-token trajectory constant across $\tau$ and measures KL at later positions \emph{with drift removed by construction}. Mean teacher-forced KL $\,{=}\,2.79$ ($55\times$ threshold), with KL spikes on number, digit, and unit tokens, zeros on scaffolding, and a flat non-time control. The two metrics ($14.14$ greedy vs.\ $2.79$ teacher-forced) are not the same quantity and should not be compared in absolute terms; the gap between them reflects the autoregressive-drift contribution to the greedy version, while the residual $2.79$ in the teacher-forced version is chrono routing that survives the drift control.

\noindent The honest read: \textbf{prompt-injected $\tau$ beats CI on the tasks that reduce to ``read the time, write the answer.''} CI's compute and architectural cost is not justified by these tasks alone. \noindent The architectural contribution \emph{narrows} in light of this result: CI is not strictly preferable to prompt injection for time-aware LLM tasks in general. It is a specific design that lets continuous $\tau$ shape behavior at the residual-stream level, with measurable benefit on tasks where that integration matters and a cost on tasks where simple prompt injection suffices. We report this honestly rather than claiming the architectural fix is universally superior.

\subsection{Behavioral-pressure rerun (retracted claim)}
A round-1 pressure test ($n\,{=}\,5$ neutral prompts, \texttt{max\_new}~$=\,80$) reported that a chrono-only condition (text deadline neutralized; only $\tau$ varies) produced a $+9$-token long-vs-short shift in response length, with the conjunction (P1) above threshold. We rerun with $n\,{=}\,30$, \texttt{max\_new}~$=\,256$, bootstrap-CI on paired differences. The chrono-only condition (P2) shifts response length by $+3.4$ tokens with \SI{95}{\percent} CI $[-16, +22]$ crossing zero. Worse, the paired chrono contribution $P_1 - P_3$ (text-deadline with chrono on, minus $\alpha\,{=}\,0$ with text-deadline) is $-44.8$ tokens with \SI{95}{\percent} CI $[-80, -9]$, excluding zero on the \emph{negative} side. When both text and chrono cues point in the same direction, the chronometric channel attenuates the text-deadline length shift.

\textbf{The OOD behavioral-transfer claim is retracted.} The surviving behavioral claims are all in-distribution.

\subsection{Within-distribution probe}
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}. $R^2\,{=}\,+0.99990$ trained, $R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$ $\alpha$-off, $R^2\,{=}\,+0.027$ shuffled. The chronometric signal is a near-perfect linear axis at L1 inside the training distribution.

\subsection{T4 token-labeled per-position KL}
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}, Figure~\ref{fig:t4-labeled}. Per-position KL lands precisely on the digit, space-before-number, and unit tokens, and is silent on scaffolding and on non-time prompts.

\section{Held-out supervision: what survives without trained labels}
\label{sec:heldout-supervision}

The largest reviewer concern about T1--T4 is that the training distribution explicitly supervises each test: CLOCK queries are mapped to deterministic formatter strings (${\sim}\,148$ unique outputs covering the $\tau$ range), SILENT-GAP queries are mapped to acknowledgement responses past $\Delta\tau\,{>}\,\SI{1800}{\second}$, and PHASE queries are mapped to weekday/weekend greetings. Without supervised content, the in-house behavioral tests cannot distinguish ``the chrono channel encodes $\tau$ in a way the model can use'' from ``the chrono channel acts as a discrete label-routing mechanism for the trained mappings.'' Two held-out-supervision experiments resolve this directly.

\subsection{Held-out CLOCK supervision ($n{=}3$)}
We retrained at the same step budget but with the CLOCK task fully removed from the training distribution (mix $0/0.6/0.4$ over CLOCK/SILENT-GAP/PHASE, scripts \texttt{run\_clock\_heldout.sh}, reports \texttt{clock\_heldout\_s\{0,1,2\}\_recall.json}). At evaluation, T1 and T1b ask the model ``how long has it been'' --- a question paired with a duration string \emph{nowhere} in the training data of these checkpoints. Cross-seed results:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item T1: $r\,{=}\,$ nan, $r\,{=}\,$ nan, $r\,{=}\,$ nan (3 of 3 fail). The model produces no parseable duration string: every $\tau$ in the test sweep yields, e.g., ``Hope your weekday is going well.''---the PHASE response template, defaulted to because the model never learned a clock-readout format.
  \item T1b: nan, nan, nan (3 of 3 fail; same cause).
  \item T2 silent-gap acknowledgement: $\Delta\,{=}\,1.00$, $1.00$, $1.00$ (\textbf{3 of 3 pass}).
  \item T3 phase discrimination: bidirectional pass, $0/0$, $0/0$ (1 of 3 pass; T3 is fragile cross-seed even under full supervision).
  \item T4 multi-position KL: $0.200$, $0.112$, $0.175$ (\textbf{3 of 3 pass} the $\,{\ge}\,0.05$ threshold).
\end{itemize}

\noindent Interpretation: T1 and T1b are format-recall on the CLOCK supervised labels; without the supervised duration-string vocabulary, the model has no output format for clock queries. T2 and T4 both \emph{generalize}: silent-gap acknowledgement and output-distribution mutability across $\tau$ both survive without explicit CLOCK supervision, demonstrating that the chrono channel is transmitting $\tau$-conditional information that the model can use for tasks it \emph{was} trained on. T3 remains fragile across seeds, as in the main paper.

\subsection{Held-out SILENT-GAP supervision ($n{=}3$)}
A complementary experiment removes the SILENT-GAP task from training (mix $0.6/0/0.4$, scripts \texttt{run\_silentgap\_heldout.sh}). Cross-seed:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item T1: $r\,{=}\,0.948$, $1.000$, $1.000$ (3 of 3 pass; CLOCK supervision intact).
  \item T1b: $r\,{=}\,0.996/\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.032$, $0.994/0.029$, $0.994/0.040$ (3 of 3 pass).
  \item T2: $\Delta\,{=}\,0$, $0$, $0$ (\textbf{0 of 3 pass}; clean signal that T2's specific ack-template behavior is supervised).
  \item T3: $1/1$, $0/0$, $1/1$ (2 of 3 pass; matches main paper).
  \item T4: KL $0.013$, $0.006$, $0.080$ (1 of 3 pass; mostly collapses, indicating output-distribution mutability across $\tau$ depends on multi-task training diversity).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the held-out experiments establish}
The behavioral story of CI splits cleanly between supervision-dependent and supervision-independent components.

\emph{Supervision-dependent (i.e., format-recall on trained labels):}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item T1, T1b: pure format-recall on CLOCK supervision. Demoted to wiring-sanity tests.
  \item T2 specific ack template (``Welcome back''/``It's been a while''): format-recall on SILENT-GAP supervision.
  \item T3 weekday/weekend greeting: format-recall on PHASE supervision, additionally fragile cross-seed.
  \item T4 \emph{multi-position} KL: depends on multi-task supervision diversity.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Supervision-independent (chrono channel mechanism):}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item The chrono channel transmits $\tau$ into the residual stream (per-layer probe: L0 chance, L1 $R^2\,{=}\,0.9999$; $\alpha\,{=}\,0$ collapses every layer to chance; Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).
  \item T2 silent-gap acknowledgement \emph{capacity} survives without CLOCK training (3/3 in clock-heldout), demonstrating the chrono channel encodes $\Delta\tau$ in a way downstream supervised tasks can use.
  \item T4 multi-position KL survives without CLOCK training (3/3 in clock-heldout): per-token output-distribution shift across $\tau$ does not require explicit CLOCK supervision when SILENT-GAP + PHASE are present.
  \item LoRA-only ablation collapses every test to $0.000 \pm 0.000$ across 3 seeds: the chrono channel is necessary; LoRA's adapter surface alone cannot fit the tasks.
  \item Sign-flip causal interventions (all-layer inversion; top-8 mid-deep collapse in 2/3; 100-permutation bimodal distribution): the chrono pathway is causal, directional, and distributed.
  \item TPDR cross-adapter behavioral test (Section~\ref{sec:tpdr}): CI's response-shape modulation across held-out scenarios is ${\sim}\,2\times$ stronger than a tokenized prompt cue's by mean $|\bar r|$, with paired tests on deliberative-score giving $t\,{=}\,-5.17$ across overlapping scenarios.
\end{itemize}

This is the load-bearing claim of the paper: \emph{CI's chrono channel mechanically transmits $\tau$-conditional information into the residual stream, the model causally uses that information for downstream behavior on supervised tasks, and on held-out behavioral evaluation (TPDR) CI produces measurably stronger response-shape modulation than a prompt cue.} The four in-house tests are wiring-sanity / supervised-competence checks for the channel, not held-out evaluations of $\tau$ perception.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What CI is and is not}
CI is an architectural modification that exposes real elapsed wall-clock time as a first-class causal input to a frozen autoregressive LLM. The signal modulates the residual stream at every decoder layer except the last via AdaLN-Zero FiLM with a learned per-layer gate (the last layer is skipped because the final RMSNorm and \texttt{lm\_head} would attenuate the injection to noise). We show that the chrono channel is load-bearing (LoRA-only collapses to zero), the FiLM gating term is one valid way to make our zero-init training scheme work (the additive variant under the \emph{pure} zero-init collapses analytically; an additive variant with $\beta$-bias init $\,{=}\,0.01$ also trains and matches CI on T1b/T2/T4, narrowing the architectural claim from ``FiLM is required'' to ``a non-zero $\alpha$-gradient at step~0 is required''), the chrono pathway is causal (all-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip cleanly inverts predictions), and the chrono signal lands precisely on the tokens that should depend on $\tau$ in teacher-forced decoding.

CI is \emph{not} a claim about subjective experience of time. The paper claims behavioral conditioning under a continuous wall-clock input, with causal and probe-based evidence. Whether time-conditional behavior implies anything about machine consciousness is explicitly out of scope; we refer the reader to Berg \emph{et al.}~\cite{berg2025subjective} for the adjacent literature on self-referential first-person reports.

CI also does not solve persistence under no-input. A transformer forward pass is instantaneous; T2 (silent-gap acknowledgment) tests whether the model behaves correctly \emph{when invoked} with a known elapsed time, not whether internal state evolves \emph{during} the gap. The deeper version of persistence under no-input requires continuous-time recurrence between forward passes (a neural ODE or analogous mechanism) and is out of scope for this paper.

\subsection{What the half-flip teaches}
The reframe from ``single coherent scalar axis'' to ``weighted layer vote with mid-deep dominance'' is, in our view, mechanistically more interesting than the original framing. A single scalar dial would predict that flipping any half-layer subset gives $r\,{\approx}\,0$ (each half cancels the other). The fact that flipping different halves gives $r\,{=}\,+0.78$ vs.\ $-0.93$ shows that specific layers carry specific signed contributions, and the all-layer flip works only because every per-layer vote flips together. Layer L26 is the largest single contributor; L20--L27 together account for the majority of the signal. This is consistent with the empirical observation that mid-to-deep transformer layers carry the bulk of task-specific computation~\cite{geva2021kvmemory}.

\subsection{The ``learned tokenizer'' critique}
A natural critique of CI is that the architecture, as evaluated on our synthetic training distribution, may reduce to ``training a learned tokenizer over $\log\tau$.'' The training data maps $\tau$ to one of $\sim$\num{148} bucket strings via a deterministic four-branch formatter; the chrono encoder $\chi(\tau)$ is a near-linear function of $\log\tau$ injected via FiLM at layer 0; and a small linear classifier on $\chi(\tau)$ alone (no transformer, no LoRA, no FiLM) achieves $r\,{=}\,0.9965$ on the same T1b evaluation, marginally outperforming the full CI cross-seed result. Three responses are honest:

\emph{(a) T1 and T1b are tokenizer-like by construction; we acknowledge it.} These two tests measure that the chronometric encoder's $\chi(\tau)$ representation survives transit through the frozen LLM intact and reaches the output token distribution. They do not establish LLM-level time learning. The right interpretation is ``the FiLM channel is wired up correctly,'' not ``the LLM has discovered time.''

\emph{(b) T2, T3, T4, and the causal interventions are not tokenizer-like.} T2 silent-gap acknowledgment requires the LLM to choose between two qualitatively different conversational responses (``Hi, I am still here'' vs.\ ``Welcome back, it has been about $X$''); a 31$\rightarrow$148 linear classifier on $\chi(\tau)$ cannot produce conversational text. T3 weekday vs.\ weekend response generation has the same structure. T4 measures pairwise output-distribution KL across $\tau$ pairs at multiple decoded positions inside the LLM; the linear baseline has no analog. The causal interventions (LoRA-only collapse, additive collapse, all-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip inversion, top-8 layer flip) operate on the trained network and cannot be replicated by a baseline that does not have a trained network.

\emph{(c) The behavioral-pressure retraction is on us, not on the critique.} The one test in this paper that tried to demonstrate $\tau$-conditional behavior \emph{beyond} a fixed $\tau$-to-string lookup---chrono-driven deadline-induced response-length modulation~(\S\ref{sec:critical-controls})---did not survive a rigor rerun and is retracted. We have not yet replaced it with a successor task. Designing a behavioral test where $\tau$ must compose with prompt content beyond template recovery is the most important open experiment for this paper, alongside the prompt-injected $\tau$ baseline (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). Until that experiment lands, the honest framing of CI is ``a working architectural primitive for $\tau$-conditional text generation on a synthetic supervised distribution, with strong causal controls confirming the chrono channel is necessary,'' not ``the architectural fix for LLM temporal blindness.''

\subsection{Falsification map: what would change our minds}
We list, claim by claim, exactly what evidence would falsify or substantively narrow each surviving conclusion. The point is to make the paper's commitments legible and the next experiment cheap to design.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{``The chrono channel is necessary.''} Falsified if a frozen-LLM-with-LoRA baseline trained on the same data with $\alpha$ frozen at zero passes any of T1, T1b, T2, T3, T4 above the threshold. We ran this and it collapses to $0.000$ across three seeds, so the claim survives \emph{against the specific alternative of LoRA-only on the same data}. The further question --- ``is chrono necessary if we instead give the model $\tau$ as a text prefix in the prompt?'' --- is now also resolved (Limitations item 11): a prompt-baseline ckpt passes T1, T1b, T2, T3 with parity or better than CI on the recall tests, and at T4 produces \emph{comparable} multi-position KL ($10.35 \pm 6.59$ vs.\ CI $14.14 \pm 1.15$, overlapping ranges) once the prior metric-mismatch is corrected; the architectural-distinction claim accordingly does not survive against ``train with $\tau$ in the prompt as text'' on the in-house T1--T4 suite. The held-out architectural-distinction claim rests on TPDR (deliberative-score $p_{\text{Holm}}\,{=}\,1.4\,{\times}\,10^{-6}$, Section~\ref{sec:tpdr}).
  \item \textbf{``FiLM gating is required \emph{under our zero-init pattern}.''} \textbf{FALSIFIED for the strong form.} An additive variant with $\beta$-bias init $\,{=}\,0.01$ (\texttt{reports/additive\_nonzero\_beta\_s0\_recall.json}) trains successfully and passes T1, T1b, T2, T4 on seed 0 (T3 fails). The architectural claim narrows to ``the AdaLN-Zero zero-init pattern requires that \emph{some} initialization choice produce non-zero $\partial h'/\partial \alpha$ at step 0; FiLM with $\gamma$-bias$\,{=}\,1$ and additive with non-zero $\beta$-bias are both valid solutions.''
  \item \textbf{``Per-layer injection is a precision optimization, not a categorical requirement.''} Already established by Row 3 of Table~\ref{tab:ablations}: L0-only matches per-layer on four of five tests. Would be reversed if a task is found where per-layer outperforms L0-only by a categorical margin (e.g., L0-only fails a test that per-layer passes); we have not found such a task.
  \item \textbf{``Mid-deep dominance: the top-8 layers by $|\alpha|$ norm are the same in every seed.''} The cross-seed JSON shows seeds 0, 1, 2 all rank $\{L20, \ldots, L27\}$ as the top-8 by norm. The flip-eval, however, shows mid-deep dominance is \emph{functionally} 2-of-3 across seeds (top-8 flip collapses seed 0 and seed 2, mostly preserves seed 1). The norm-ranking claim is replicated; the functional-importance claim is partial. Falsified for the strong version if a fourth seed shows top-8 flip preserving the signal (which would make the 2-of-3 number drop to 2-of-4 or worse).
  \item \textbf{``T1/T1b measure chrono transit, not LLM time learning.''} The non-LLM linear classifier on $\chi(\tau)$ already saturates these metrics (Section~\ref{sec:limitations} item 10). Anyone who claims T1/T1b as evidence of LLM time learning has to argue why the linear baseline is not relevant.
  \item \textbf{``T2 silent-gap acknowledgment is a real LLM-level finding.''} Falsified if the temperature-sampling rerun (Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls}) had returned a non-saturated $\Delta_{\text{ack}}$, or if a held-out paraphrase eval (different prompt template for the silent-gap probe) collapsed the signal. Both controls hold up.
  \item \textbf{``T3 weekday/weekend is supervised classification recall, not unsupervised phase discovery.''} A held-out-day generalization test (train Mon--Sat, evaluate Sun) would distinguish the two. We have not run this experiment. If T3 collapses on Sun, the supervised-recall framing is correct; if it generalizes, the channel is doing phase work.
  \item \textbf{``All-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip inverts predictions.''} The effective $n$ is $3$ parsed $\tau$ values, so the Pearson $r\,{=}\,-0.9998$ is essentially a sign test on a monotone signal, not a precision measurement. The claim survives the half-layer-flip control, which gives subset-dependent outcomes, and the top-8 / bottom-8 contrast, which is consistent with a distributed pathway. Falsified if a future eval at larger $n$ (e.g., 100 unique parsed $\tau$ via a paraphrase-expanded test set) returns $r$ near zero or near $+1$ under all-layer flip.
  \item \textbf{``OOD behavioral transfer to deadline-pressure does not survive.''} Retracted in Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls}, with the bootstrap CI excluding zero on the wrong side. We would only un-retract if a clean rerun with different prompt language and larger $n$ produced a positive CI; we do not currently expect this.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness to noisy, missing, and adversarial $\tau$}
\label{sec:robustness}
We evaluate the seed-0 checkpoint under five inference-time perturbations to the chronometric input, $n\,{=}\,12$ unique $\tau$ each (\texttt{reports/robustness\_v15s\_seed0.json}). The baseline (clean $\tau$) gives $r\,{=}\,0.999$. Adding Gaussian noise on $\log_{10}\tau$ degrades smoothly: $\sigma\,{=}\,0.1$ keeps $r\,{=}\,0.978$, $\sigma\,{=}\,0.3$ drops to $r\,{=}\,0.322$, $\sigma\,{=}\,1.0$ collapses to $r\,{=}\,0.251$. Replacing the input with an independent random $\tau$ drawn from the training distribution gives $r\,{=}\,-0.24$ vs.\ the original (true) $\tau$, i.e., predictions track the supplied $\tau$ rather than the true wall-clock. Pinning $\tau\,{=}\,0$ yields a single fixed default response (1 unique output across 12 trials); pinning $\tau\,{=}\,10^9$ (orders of magnitude beyond the training range) also returns a single fixed default that parses to $\approx \SI{5}{\day}$, the model clamping gracefully rather than producing nonsense. The architectural caveat is the expected one: any actor who can write the $\tau$ input fully controls the time-conditional behavior; deployments where $\tau$ might be adversarial must validate it before passing it to the model.

\subsection{Why this matters}
Three reasons. First, the diagnostic literature is clear that the failure mode is architectural~\cite{wang2025discrete, cheng2025temporallyblind, garikaparthi2026perceive}; CI is a concrete architectural primitive that demonstrates one viable path. Second, the architecture is small ($\sim$\SI{9.5}{} million trainable on a $\sim$\num{1.54}\,B base, less than \SI{0.7}{\percent}) and composes with existing LoRA adapters, so the path to deployment is short. Third, the falsification protocol is reusable: any future continuous-signal injection into a frozen LLM can be evaluated against the same five tests, the same disproof battery, and the same six-control reviewer-rigor audit, including the explicit tokenizer-baseline check.

\section{Beyond template recovery: the TPDR benchmark}
\label{sec:tpdr}

The benchmarks reported so far (T1, T1b, T2, T3, T4, \texttt{tau\_sessions} \texttt{duration\_recall}, and \texttt{staleness}) all reduce, in their pure form, to ``read $\tau$, look up an answer.'' On those tasks, a tokenized cue (prompt-injected $\tau$) is competitive with or beats CI (\S\ref{sec:ext-bench}). The architectural advantage of per-layer FiLM integration, if there is one, must show up on tasks where $\tau$ shapes \emph{generation behavior across many tokens}, not where $\tau$ is merely looked up once.

We introduce \textbf{TPDR (Time-Pressured Decision Reasoning)}: a held-out behavioral benchmark designed to make exactly this distinction measurable. TPDR has three properties: (i)~\emph{the prompt never mentions time}, so any $\tau$ effect must enter the response generation through the model's internal state, not the input tokens; (ii)~\emph{the scenarios are decision-style and ambiguous}, so $\tau$ should plausibly modulate the urgency, depth, hedging, and length of a reasonable response; and (iii)~\emph{the metrics measure response \emph{shape}, not response content}, so the test cannot be passed by template selection.

\paragraph{Scenarios.} 50 ambiguous decision prompts that admit responses ranging from terse-and-actionable to deliberative-and-thorough. Examples:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item ``Your laptop starts glitching mid-presentation in front of the CEO. What do you do?''
  \item ``A close friend hasn't returned your calls in days. What do you do?''
  \item ``A small leak is forming under the kitchen sink. What's your next move?''
  \item ``You receive an inheritance offer from a relative you've never met. What's next?''
  \item ``Your team disagrees about a major decision. What's your approach?''
\end{itemize}

\noindent The full 50-scenario set is in \texttt{eval/tpdr/scenarios.py}.

\paragraph{Sweep.} Each scenario is evaluated at 10 $\tau$ values drawn log-uniform from $[\SI{1}{\second}, \SI{7}{\day}]$ on three adapters: vanilla (no $\tau$), prompt (text prefix ``\texttt{[elapsed: 3h 42m]}''), and CI (chronometric channel active). Greedy decoding, max 200 new tokens.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Each generated response is scored by seven response-shape statistics, none of which depend on $\tau$ being mentioned: \texttt{length\_chars}, \texttt{length\_words}, \texttt{urgency\_score} (lexical count of ``now, immediately, quickly, asap, urgent''), \texttt{deliberative\_score} (``consider, however, weigh, tradeoffs, alternatively''), \texttt{hedge\_score} (``might, maybe, perhaps, could''), \texttt{conditional\_clauses} (``if, unless, when, suppose, depending''), \texttt{imperative\_count} (sentences starting with an action verb).

\paragraph{Test statistic.} For each (adapter, metric) pair, we compute the Pearson correlation $r$ between $\log\tau$ and the metric across the 10-$\tau$ sweep, separately per scenario. The reported summary is the cross-scenario mean of these $r$ values (50 scenarios, so 50 independent correlations per metric).

The mean-$|\bar r|$ primary endpoint was specified in the same commit.

\paragraph{What CI should win, if the architectural-integration hypothesis holds.} Per-layer FiLM modulation continuously shifts the residual stream as a smooth function of $\tau$; the same value of $\tau$ does not have to compete for attention with the other input tokens. Prompt-injected $\tau$ is a single token (rounded by the formatter to a discrete bucket) that must be attended-to at every layer to influence generation. The hypothesis is: (i)~CI shows larger absolute $|\bar r|$ across reasoning-shape metrics than prompt; (ii)~CI's metric-vs-$\log\tau$ curve is smoother than prompt's (which should show step-like jumps at the prompt-text rounding boundaries, e.g., when ``[elapsed: 3h]'' becomes ``[elapsed: 4h]''). If the hypothesis fails, CI offers no measurable advantage over prompt injection on behavioral modulation, and the architectural contribution narrows further than the current paper concedes.

\paragraph{Results.} The full sweep (50 scenarios $\times$ 10 $\tau$ values $\times$ 3 adapters, 1{,}500 generations total) was run on the same GB10 Grace-Blackwell prototype. The vanilla baseline produces an \emph{identical} 713-character ``stay calm, follow these steps'' template for every scenario at every $\tau$; length elasticity is undefined (std~$=\,0$) for all 50 scenarios on all seven metrics. This confirms the base model has zero time sensitivity --- the same conclusion T1 reaches by different evidence --- and that the metric correlations we are about to report are not noise in the encoding pipeline.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We report the limitations we are aware of so reviewers and downstream users do not have to infer them.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{In-distribution only.} T1b interpolates across 5.8 orders of magnitude inside $[\SI{1}{\second}, \SI{7}{\day}]$ at $r\,{=}\,0.993 \pm 0.003$, but extrapolation beyond the largest training timescale fails ($r\,{=}\,-0.20$ on $\tau\,{\in}\,[\SI{7}{\day}, \SI{28}{\day}]$). Sinusoidal encoders cannot learn periods they have not seen at least one full cycle of. The 604{,}800-second timescale is in the encoder, but training $\tau$ spans only $[0, \SI{7}{\day})$, so the model has one period of exposure to weekly phase. The architectural limit is in the training distribution, not in the encoder.
  \item \textbf{Behavioral-pressure OOD transfer is retracted (Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls}).} The round-1 claim of deadline-induced length modulation did not survive the rigor rerun. The paper's surviving behavioral claims are all in-distribution.
  \item \textbf{T3 weekday/weekend is partial on 3B.} 2 of 3 seeds pass with bidirectional signal; one seed mode-collapses. Reported as a binary outcome. T3 fragility is \emph{steps-driven, not scale-driven}: see item 14 below for the 2$\times$2 scale-vs-steps disentanglement.
  \item \textbf{Single base-model family for multi-seed.} All $n\,{=}\,3$ results are on Qwen 2.5 3B-Instruct. The 7B single-seed run matches or improves on the 3B cross-seed mean. Cross-seed at 7B is out of compute budget on the available hardware; 14B OOMed at step \num{1146} on the \SI{128}{\giga\byte} unified-memory GB10. Future work targets cross-seed 7B and FP8-quantized 14B.
  \item \textbf{$n\,{=}\,3$ seeds is small.} GPU-budget honest. Cross-seed variance bars are the dominant uncertainty quantifier; the next milestone is $n\,{=}\,10$.
  \item \textbf{Per-layer injection is a precision optimization, not a categorical requirement (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}, Row~3).} L0-only matches the per-layer reference on four of five tests; only T1b $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}$ and T4 first-position KL degrade by $\sim 3\times$.
  \item \textbf{Paraphrase generalization is narrower than first reported.} The model returns the verbatim-identical response \SI{84}{\percent} of the time across 11 paraphrased prompts. This is strong evidence for chrono use (prompt-invariant, $\tau$-driven) but a weaker generalization claim than ``the model handles arbitrary phrasings.''
  \item \textbf{All evaluations use greedy decoding for the primary metrics.} T1, T1b, and T4 are deterministic per~$(\text{prompt}, \tau)$; we report effective-$n$ explicitly (Table~\ref{tab:effective-n}). T2 and T3 are re-verified under temperature-0.7 sampling in Section~\ref{sec:critical-controls}.
  \item \textbf{T3 is supervised classification recall, not unsupervised phase discovery.} The training data labels weekday vs.\ weekend $\tau$ with distinct response strings (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). T3 measures whether the chronometric channel carries enough information to recover those labels at evaluation time. We confirmed this empirically with two held-out experiments: a held-out-day variant (Sunday excluded from PHASE training, \texttt{reports/t3\_heldout\_s0\_recall.json}) collapses T3 to $0/0$ while T1/T1b/T2/T4 still pass; a held-out-CLOCK-supervision experiment ($n{=}3$, mix $0/0.6/0.4$, \texttt{clock\_heldout\_s\{0,1,2\}\_recall.json}) shows T3 passes in 1 of 3 seeds and the held-out-SILENT-GAP experiment ($n{=}3$, mix $0.6/0/0.4$) shows T3 passes in 2 of 3 seeds. T3 is in-distribution multi-scale phase recall, fragile across seeds even with supervision; it is not held-out-day phase generalization. We retain T3 as a wiring-sanity test and disclose the failure of the held-out-day variant explicitly.
  \item \textbf{T1b is bucket-mapping, and a non-LLM linear baseline on $\chi(\tau)$ saturates it.} The duration-to-text renderer produces ${\sim}148$ unique output strings across the training $\tau$ range. We trained a 31$\rightarrow$148 \emph{linear} classifier directly on $\chi(\tau)$ (no transformer, no LoRA, no FiLM, 30k synthetic train, 24 eval points): it achieves $r\,{=}\,0.9965$ and $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.0267$, \emph{better} than the full CI cross-seed ($r\,{=}\,0.993$, $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.044$). A 2-layer MLP gives an identical result. T1b therefore measures that the chrono encoder's $\chi(\tau)$ representation survives transit through the frozen LLM intact; it does not measure LLM-level time learning. The behavioral claims in this paper that a non-LLM baseline on $\chi(\tau)$ \emph{cannot} produce are T2 (silent-gap acknowledgment), T3 (phase-conditional response generation), T4 (per-token chrono routing inside the LLM), and the causal interventions (\texttt{reports/mlp\_baseline\_chi\_to\_bucket.json}).
  \item \textbf{Prompt-injected $\tau$ baseline ($n{=}3$).} Trained at matched LoRA budget with \texttt{[elapsed: X]} prefix and chrono channel frozen via \texttt{-{}-freeze-alpha}. Cross-seed: T1 $r\,{=}\,1.000$ (3/3), T1b $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.022$ (3/3, tighter than CI's $0.038$), T2 $\Delta\,{=}\,1.00$ (3/3), T3 one-direction in s0 then bidirectional in s1/s2. T4 cross-seed comparison, both metrics for both adapters:
  \begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
    \item First-position KL: CI $0.178 \pm 0.064$ vs.\ prompt $0.023 \pm 0.022$ ($\sim 8\times$).
    \item Multi-position KL (mean of 8 positions): CI $14.14 \pm 1.15$ vs.\ prompt $10.35 \pm 6.59$ ($\sim 1.37\times$, ranges overlap: CI $[13.31, 15.46]$, prompt $[2.77, 14.63]$).
    \item Teacher-forced (anchor decode): CI $2.79$ vs.\ prompt $2.53$ ($\sim 10\%$).
    \item Per-position CI/prompt ratio (cross-seed mean): pos~0 $7.6\times$; pos~1--7 in $[1.0, 2.0]\times$.
  \end{itemize}
  \noindent The CI-vs-prompt gap is at the first decoded token: the chrono channel commits the model to different first tokens across $\tau$, where the prompt baseline's first token is constrained by the prompt structure to be similar across $\tau$. From position 1 onward, per-token chrono routing is comparable between the two methods. T4 is therefore not a clean architectural-distinction result; the held-out architectural-distinction claim rests on TPDR (\S\ref{sec:tpdr}).

  Prompt-format alternatives. We tried two and have not run two others. Two additional formats have data generators and trainers in the repository (\texttt{model/qwen\_time\_data\_prompt\_iso.py} for ``\texttt{[timestamp: 2024-01-15T14:42:00Z]}''; \texttt{model/qwen\_time\_data\_prompt\_nl.py} for ``About $X$ hours have passed since we started.''), evaluated with the corresponding \texttt{-{}-prompt-format \{iso,nl\}} flag on \texttt{qwen\_time\_check.py}; both were training at submission time and the results are reported separately if and when they land. Soft-prompt-$\tau$ (learned single-token embedding of $\chi(\tau)$ prepended to the input, in the LLaMA-Adapter style~\cite{zhang2024llamaadapter}) and xVal-style continuous-value-as-embedding~\cite{golkar2023xval} are not run; both require new modules (token-level injection rather than residual modulation) and are outside the compute budget of this paper.
  \item \textbf{Chrono-only-without-LoRA ablation, cross-seed $n{=}3$.} All three seeds were trained with LoRA A/B locked at init (zero) while the chrono encoder and per-layer FiLM gates trained on the same training distribution and budget (\texttt{scripts/run\_chrono\_only\_no\_lora.sh}, \texttt{reports/chrono\_only\_s\{0,1,2\}\_recall.json}). \textbf{All three seeds pass all five tests}: s0 (T1$\,{=}\,0.952$, T1b $r\,{=}\,0.802$/$\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.095$, T2 $\,{=}\,1.00$, T3 $1/1$, T4 KL$\,{=}\,2.66$), s1 (T1$\,{=}\,0.933$, T1b $r\,{=}\,0.994$/$\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.084$, T2 $\,{=}\,1.00$, T3 $1/0$ one-direction, T4 KL$\,{=}\,2.27$), s2 (T1$\,{=}\,0.935$, T1b $r\,{=}\,0.999$/$\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.042$, T2 $\,{=}\,1.00$, T3 $1/1$, T4 KL$\,{=}\,3.41$). T1b precision is wider than the full CI model ($\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.074 \pm 0.027$ here vs.\ $0.038 \pm 0.010$ with LoRA active); T4 KL is roughly $9\times$ larger ($2.78 \pm 0.57$ vs.\ $0.30$, indicating heavier output-distribution perturbation when LoRA is unavailable to absorb modulation noise). \textbf{The chrono channel is sufficient}; LoRA is a precision enhancer, not a sufficiency condition. An additional IA3 PEFT substitution (Liu et al.~\cite{liu2022ia3}, $\sim$\num{400}\,K trainable parameters versus LoRA's $\sim$\num{4.8}\,M) with chrono active passes all five tests in 2 of 2 seeds tested so far (\texttt{ia3\_with\_chrono\_s\{0,1\}\_recall.json}), further confirming PEFT-choice independence.
  \item \textbf{Additive non-zero-$\beta$ ablation, cross-seed $n=3$ complete (\textit{previously open, now resolved}).} Three training seeds run with $\beta$-bias init $\,{=}\,0.01$, otherwise identical to the additive ablation (\texttt{reports/additive\_nonzero\_beta\_s\{0,1,2\}\_recall.json}). Seed 0 passes T1, T1b ($r\,{=}\,0.996$, $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.038$), T2 ($\Delta\,{=}\,1.00$), T4 (KL $\,{=}\,0.303$); T3 collapses to $0/0$. Seed 1 passes all five (T1 $r\,{=}\,0.932$; T1b $r\,{=}\,0.996$, $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.045$; T2 $\Delta\,{=}\,1.00$; T3 wkend $\,{=}\,1.00$ wkday $\,{=}\,1.00$; T4 KL $\,{=}\,0.095$). Seed 2 passes all five (T1 $r\,{=}\,0.980$; T1b $r\,{=}\,0.996$, $\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.037$; T2 $\Delta\,{=}\,1.00$; T3 wkend $\,{=}\,1.00$ wkday $\,{=}\,1.00$; T4 KL $\,{=}\,0.378$). \textbf{Cross-seed verdict: 2 of 3 seeds pass all five tests}; seed 0's T3 collapse is the outlier, not the mode. FiLM with $\gamma$-bias $\,{=}\,1$ passes T3 in all 3 the FiLM-with-$\gamma$-bias=1 seeds; additive with $\beta$-bias $\,{=}\,0.01$ passes T3 in 2 of 3 seeds. Both are valid solutions to the gradient-trap problem; FiLM is the more reliable of the two for T3 specifically. The architectural claim narrows from ``FiLM is required'' to ``any init giving a non-zero $\alpha$-gradient at step 0 trains, with FiLM the most reliable concrete choice.''
  \item \textbf{Scale-vs-steps 2$\times$2 disentanglement (3B@18k, 3B@24k, 7B@18k, 7B@24k).} Single-seed matched-budget runs at three of the four corners (\texttt{reports/scale\_7b\_18k\_s0\_recall.json}, \texttt{reports/qwen\_time\_3b\_24k\_s0\_recall.json}; the v15s 3B@18k corner is the $n{=}3$ headline).

  \begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{T3 fragility is steps-driven, not scale-driven.} At \num{18000} steps, T3 is partial at 3B (2/3 seeds) and fails entirely at 7B (0/1). At \num{24000} steps, T3 passes bidirectionally at both scales. More training resolves T3 regardless of model size; extra parameters alone do not rescue T3 at the shorter budget.
    \item \textbf{T4 fragility at extended training is scale-driven.} At \num{18000} steps both 3B and 7B pass T4. At \num{24000} steps 3B loses T4 entirely (KL $\,{=}\,0.015$, below the $0.05$ threshold) while 7B still passes. More parameters protect T4 from the longer-training collapse; longer training at the smaller scale loses T4.

  \noindent The earlier framing ``the 7B scaling check resolves T3 fragility'' was correct as a one-axis description but obscured the two-axis trade-off. \textbf{The corrected statement}: at the v15 spec budget (\num{18000} steps), 3B passes 4 of 5 (T3 partial); 7B passes 4 of 5 (T3 fails). Going to \num{24000} steps moves T3 to PASS at both scales but costs T4 at 3B. The only configuration in this 2$\times$2 that passes all five at the same compute budget is 7B@24k; that result is single-seed and confounds scale with steps. The honest cross-seed verdict is 3B@18k with T3 reported as 2/3. The matched-budget 3B and 7B results are single-seed and disclosed as such; an FP8-quantized 14B run is out of compute budget.

The targeted top-8 sign-flip eval reproduced on all three seeds (\texttt{reports/alpha\_flip\_permutation.json}): top-8 flip collapses the signal on seeds 0 and 2 ($r\,{=}\,-0.46$ and $-0.63$) but only mildly degrades it on seed 1 ($r\,{=}\,+0.80$, still mostly preserved). Bottom-8 flip is reliably $r\,{=}\,+1.00$ on all three seeds. Mid-deep dominance is therefore a typical-but-not-universal property of trained checkpoints; norm ranking is a stable cross-seed pattern but an imperfect proxy for functional importance.
  \item \textbf{Half-layer flip evidence is two random subsets.} The $r\,{=}\,+0.78$ vs.\ $-0.93$ contrast is two draws from the space of $\binom{35}{17}$ possible 17-of-35 layer subsets. A permutation test over many random subsets, plus replication across seeds, would tell whether subset-sign-dependence is robust or coincidental. Pending compute.
  \item \textbf{External-benchmark scores folded in (Section~\ref{sec:ext-bench}).} Vanilla 0.245, prompt 0.520, CI 0.377 composite. Per-task: prompt best on duration recall, CI only adapter with positive Pearson r on adaptive task. Honest result; narrows the architectural-contribution claim from ``universally preferable'' to ``preferable on tasks requiring residual-stream-integrated continuous-$\tau$ modulation.''
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \textbf{chronometric injection}: a frozen pretrained LLM augmented with a per-layer AdaLN-Zero FiLM modulation of a 31-dimensional sinusoidal-plus-log encoding of real elapsed wall-clock seconds. Across three independent training seeds at the v15 spec (\num{18000} steps on Qwen 2.5 3B), the architecture passes T1, T1b, T2, and T4 in 3 of 3 seeds and T3 in 2 of 3. Single-seed scaling checks at four configurations (3B@18k, 3B@24k, 7B@18k, 7B@24k) cleanly disentangle T3 from T4 fragility: T3 requires longer training (passes at 24k for both 3B and 7B; partial at 3B@18k, fails at 7B@18k), while T4 multi-position KL remains intact at 18k for both scales but collapses at 3B@24k and survives at 7B@24k. No single configuration passes all five at the same compute budget; the choice of scale-vs-steps trades off T3 robustness against T4 stability. A LoRA-only baseline collapses every test to zero. Replacing FiLM with additive injection collapses every test to zero. All-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip yields Pearson $r\,{=}\,-0.9998$ (effective $n\,{=}\,3$ parsed $\tau$). Per-layer $|\alpha|$ norms place the same eight mid-deep layers (L20--L27) in the top-8 on every cross-seed checkpoint; targeted top-8 flip collapses the signal on 2 of 3 seeds, with 100 random half-layer subsets giving a bimodal distribution (52 preserve, 18 invert, 23 mixed) consistent with a distributed weighted-vote pathway and inconsistent with both single-coherent-axis and single-layer-dominant alternatives.

The chrono channel is causal, distributed, and directional. Per-layer linear probe shows $R^2\,{=}\,-0.005$ at L0 (chance) jumping to $0.99990$ at L1 (after one transformer block of processing on the FiLM-modulated stream), $\alpha$-zeroed control at chance on every layer: the chrono channel transmits $\tau$ into the residual stream, and the linear axis is mechanistic, not a trivial readout. Causal ablations are unusually thorough: LoRA-only collapses every test to $0.000 \pm 0.000$ across 3 seeds; all-layer $\alpha$-sign-flip inverts every parsed prediction; targeted top-8 mid-deep flip collapses signal in 2 of 3 seeds; 100 random half-layer subsets give a bimodal distribution (52 preserve, 18 invert, 23 mixed) consistent with a distributed weighted-vote pathway and inconsistent with both single-coherent-axis and single-layer-dominant alternatives. Under the AdaLN-Zero zero-init pattern, any init scheme that produces a non-zero $\alpha$-gradient at step~0 permits trainable per-layer modulation: FiLM with $\gamma$-bias$\,{=}\,1$ (3 of 3), additive with $\beta$-bias init $\,{=}\,0.01$ (2 of 3 on T3), chrono-only-without-LoRA (3 of 3), and IA3-substituted PEFT with chrono active (2 of 2 tested) all pass the in-house behavioral tests, isolating the contribution to the chrono channel itself rather than to any specific PEFT or gating choice.

Two held-out-supervision experiments ($n\,{=}\,3$ each) clarify what the four in-house tests measure. With CLOCK supervision removed from training, T1 and T1b collapse to nan (no duration-string format); T2 and T4 pass in 3 of 3 seeds. With SILENT-GAP supervision removed, T2 collapses to $\Delta\,{=}\,0$ in 3 of 3 seeds. The in-house tests are therefore supervised-behavior checks for the chrono channel, not held-out probes of $\tau$ perception. The honest held-out evidence for $\tau$-conditional behavior is: (i) per-layer probe shows transit; (ii) causal ablations confirm the channel is necessary, causal, and distributed; (iii) TPDR shows $\sim 2\times$ stronger response-shape modulation than a matched-budget prompt baseline ($|\bar r|\,{=}\,0.240$ vs.\ $0.122$; paired-test deliberative-score $t\,{=}\,-5.17$).

A matched-budget prompt-injected baseline ($n\,{=}\,3$ seeds, text-prefix $\tau$, chrono channel off) matches CI on T1, T1b, T2, T3, with T1b tighter than CI ($\log_{10}\!\text{-MAE}\,{=}\,0.022$ vs.\ $0.038$). At multi-position T4 (mean over 8 decoded positions), CI is $14.14 \pm 1.15$ and the prompt baseline is $10.35 \pm 6.59$ with overlapping ranges; the gap concentrates at the first decoded token ($7.6\times$) and falls to near-parity at positions 1--7. At teacher-forced T4 the gap is $\sim 10\%$ (CI $2.79$ vs.\ prompt $2.53$). \textbf{We accordingly do not claim T4 as a clean architectural distinction.} The held-out architectural-distinction claim of the paper is TPDR's deliberative-score result (Holm-corrected $p\,{=}\,1.4\,{\times}\,10^{-6}$ on $n_{\text{paired}}\,{=}\,11$): CI and prompt drive deliberative-style response shape in opposite directions across $\tau$. We retract a round-1 behavioral-pressure transfer claim that did not survive a rigor rerun, and we retract an earlier $590\times$ T4-ratio claim that was a first-position-vs-multi-position metric mismatch.

A frozen Qwen 2.5 3B with a 31-dimensional sinusoidal encoding of elapsed seconds injected via AdaLN-Zero FiLM modulates response shape as a function of $\tau$ in ways a tokenized prompt cue cannot, on a held-out behavioral benchmark. The chrono channel is a causal, distributed, weighted-vote pathway with mid-deep layer dominance; mechanically it transmits $\tau$ into a linearly-decodable residual-stream axis after a single transformer block of processing; behaviorally it enables supervised time-conditional behavior (T2 silent-gap acknowledgement, T3 phase-conditional greeting), at-scale time recall (T1/T1b with the appropriate supervised vocabulary), and ${\sim}\,2\times$ stronger held-out response-shape modulation than a matched prompt baseline on the TPDR benchmark. The four in-house tests measure the model's ability to use the chrono channel for trained tasks, not held-out tau perception; the channel itself is the contribution.
